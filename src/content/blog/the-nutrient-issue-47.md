On October 29th, HHS and the FDA announced the first phase of a review that will revise 8 of the 30 nutrients the federal government currently requires in infant formula, part of what the agencies are calling the first major update to those standards in nearly 30 years. It's framed as one piece of Operation Stork Speed, the administration's push to overhaul infant formula oversight, and it covers iodine, manganese, vitamin A, and vitamin D, while reaffirming the existing requirements for chloride, potassium, selenium, and sodium. Two more phases, covering the rest of the 30 required nutrients, are still to come. The public comment period is open now and runs until February 26th, 2027.

This is the kind of news that lands on a very specific group of people: parents who are feeding a baby formula right now, today, and want to know whether the can in their cabinet is still fine, or whether anything about what's required in it is about to change. A lot of those parents are also trying to eat well themselves, postpartum, sleep-deprived, and looking for a straight answer from whatever app is already on their phone. So I checked what happens when one of them asks Mr Bite's coach about it.

The honest answer is: it depends on an unusual amount of luck, and even with luck, it ends somewhere thin.

Start with who the app thinks is asking. Issue 45 already covered this in detail: `mrBiteHealthSafety.ts` defines `pregnant_or_postpartum` as one of four reasons a user's coaching should change, but the only screen in the entire codebase that ever asks a user whether that's true, the conversational onboarding question in `onboardingChatQuestions.ts`, is never mounted anywhere. So the population this news is most relevant to isn't even reliably flagged as that population in the first place. That gap hasn't changed in the two days since.

Assume it didn't matter, and a postpartum user just typed her question into the chat anyway. Before the coach decides whether to attach a citation at all, two separate regexes have to agree the question is health-related: `HEALTH_QA_TOPIC_RE` in `mr-bite-chat/index.ts` and `HEALTH_QA_LANGUAGE_RE` in `mrBiteChatLoopPolicy.ts`. Both list `pregnan(?:cy|t)` by name. Neither lists "formula," "infant," "baby," or "iodine." A question like "does formula still meet the new iodine rule" only trips either gate if it happens to also contain one of the generic words both regexes do carry, like "safe" or "healthy." Say it plainly enough and it's lucky. Say it plainly and specifically, the way someone reading an actual news headline would, and it can slip through with no citation requirement attached at all.

Say it clears that gate too. The citation itself comes from `mrBiteHealthClaimSources.ts`, which tries a live, API-based web search first, restricted to an allow-list of 21 government and health-authority domains, and falls back to a deterministic list of 12 regex-matched topic groups if that search comes back empty. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, and lactation are one group among the 12, matched by `/\b(pregnan\w*|breastfeed\w*|lactation)\b/i`, and that single group returns exactly one source, every time, for every question it catches: MedlinePlus's general "Pregnancy and Nutrition" page. It's a fine page. It is about diet during pregnancy. It says nothing about formula, iodine, or infant nutrient requirements, because that was never its subject.

And the live search path, the one that's supposed to catch exactly this kind of specific, breaking question, has its own quiet failure mode. `addSource` in that same file checks each result's hostname against the allow-list with an exact match, not a subdomain match, after stripping "www." The allow-list includes `medlineplus.gov`, `pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`, and `nih.gov`, but not `ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`, the actual host of LactMed, the National Library of Medicine's own database of drugs and nutrients and their effects on breastfeeding, the single most relevant free federal source for this exact question. If the live search found it, which is plausible, since it's exactly what a good web search would return for a lactation query, `addSource` would silently drop it, because `ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` is not `pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`, string for string.

So the realistic path for a new parent asking Mr Bite about the biggest infant formula news in three decades runs through a detection gate that doesn't know the word "formula," a fallback citation that was written for a different topic, and a live-search filter that would reject the one source built for exactly this. None of this is a dramatic bug. It's three small, reasonable-looking decisions, made separately, that happen to add up to a coach with nothing real to say about the thing its most vulnerable users are most likely to ask about this week.

## Three Quick Bites

![Three vignettes in a row: a package of hot dogs and deli meat beside a small thermometer icon, a pint container of gelato beside a glowing bacterium icon, and a smoked fish fillet beside a small warning triangle icon, warm cream background with coral and soft teal accents](https://d8j0ntlcm91z4.cloudfront.net/user_3ElLNwQpO0GqKZ9IN6sdAl7YP6i/hf_20261101_081523_7de72eba-12ba-4362-8d8b-121ea8c5542d.png)

1. **Kayem Foods, of Chelsea, Massachusetts, recalled roughly 1.58 million pounds of ready-to-eat hot dogs, bacon, sausage, ham, and deli meats on October 17th after a warehouse cooler ran warm.** The problem surfaced when a retail customer complained on October 1st that products had arrived warm; USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service classified it Class I, its highest-risk tier, covering eight brands including Kayem, Al Fresco, and McKenzie, made between March 31st and October 7th. No illnesses have been confirmed. [The Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/19/business/kayem-recalls-hot-dogs-and-other-meat-products/)

2. **Lidl US recalled a single lot of Gelatelli Coffee & Vanilla Gelato with Coffee Sauce & Cookie Pieces after routine testing turned up Listeria monocytogenes.** The 1-pint containers, lot 085261 with a best-by date of March 26th, 2028, were sold across nine states and Washington, D.C. between July 19th and October 15th; no illnesses have been reported, and Lidl is offering refunds at any store location. [Fox Carolina](https://www.foxcarolina.com/2026/10/22/lidl-recalls-gelato-possible-listeria-contamination/)

3. **The FDA warned on October 23rd that cold-smoked sprats from D&M Smoked Fish, doing business as Haifa Smoked Fish, carry a botulism risk because they were produced without the controls needed to stop Clostridium botulinum growth, and the company initially declined the agency's request to recall them.** The product was sold at the retail level in New York, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania; no illnesses had been reported as of the advisory, and the FDA said it would update the alert as the situation develops. [Food Safety News](https://foodsafetynews.com/2026/10/fda-issues-warning-about-cold-smoked-fish-because-of-botulism-risk-company-refuses-to-recall-product)

## Tool of the Week: LactMed

![An open database icon branching into a small labeled bottle icon and a breastfeeding parent-and-child icon, connecting out to a simple phone app icon, warm cream background with coral and soft teal accents](https://d8j0ntlcm91z4.cloudfront.net/user_3ElLNwQpO0GqKZ9IN6sdAl7YP6i/hf_20261101_081525_94ea160c-6046-493b-a9f8-4631befcaefd.png)

Since this issue is about a citation system with nothing built for breastfeeding or infant nutrition beyond one generic pregnancy page, the pick this week is the free federal database that was built for exactly that: LactMed, the National Library of Medicine's peer-reviewed database on drugs, supplements, and other substances and their documented effects on breastfeeding, hosted on NCBI's Bookshelf. It won't answer a question about infant formula's nutrient requirements directly, that's a job for the FDA's own infant formula pages, but it's the right shape of fix for the lactation half of this gap, and it's a reminder that "on an allow-listed domain" and "actually useful for this question" aren't automatically the same thing when the allow-list is checked hostname by exact hostname. [LactMed](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501922/)
