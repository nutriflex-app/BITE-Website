On October 22nd, Calley Means, the senior HHS adviser who had been Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s loudest voice on food policy, resigned. Two administration officials told Politico the breaking point was that same week: a meeting among Means, Kennedy, and White House staff to finally define "ultra-processed food," the term at the center of the whole MAHA food agenda, got postponed again. Kennedy had promised a first-ever federal definition back in April. Means gave no reason in his own public statement, only that he leaves "with tremendous respect" for Kennedy and the President. Everyone reading the resignation is filling in the same blank he left.

I don't think the holdup is laziness. I think "ultra-processed food" is genuinely hard to define cleanly in a way that holds up once lawyers, lobbyists, and label printers get involved, and that's a sharper version of a problem Mr Bite runs into constantly at a much smaller scale: nutrition claims are hard to pin down cleanly, full stop. We never tried to build a definition of ultra-processed food into the app. The last issue I wrote covered why a single processing label is the wrong level of resolution for what actually matters in a food. But we did build something adjacent to Washington's real sticking point here, not a definition, a check: when our own coach tells someone a fact about their health, is that fact backed by something real.

That system lives in `mrBiteHealthClaimSources.ts`, and it's stricter than I expected when I went and actually read it. There's a hard allow list, thirteen domains once you dedupe the `www.` variants: who.int, nhs.uk, cdc.gov, nih.gov, medlineplus.gov, pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, fda.gov, usda.gov, myplate.gov, dietaryguidelines.gov, health.gov, nutrition.gov, and NIH's own supplement office page. Nothing else qualifies. `verifyHealthClaimsWithWebSearch` sends the claim to a model with a live web-search tool scoped to just that list, carrying instructions that read, in part, "Do not diagnose, treat, prescribe, or provide medical nutrition therapy. If evidence is mixed, narrow, or missing, say that plainly." Every link that comes back gets checked against the allow list a second time in plain code, `addSource()` throws out anything whose domain isn't on it, so a model that ignored its own instructions still couldn't sneak an unvetted source past this. And if live search comes back with nothing at all, a deterministic fallback keyword-matches the question against a dozen pre-picked MedlinePlus and NHS pages, because, as the code comment puts it, we need every health answer to carry a Sources block no matter what, which is there specifically for App Store guideline 1.4.1. That's a careful piece of engineering for what looks, from the outside, like a small helper file.

Here's the part that isn't careful enough. All of that machinery only runs when `isHealthQATurnNeedingSources()` decides the turn needs it, and the gate inside that function, `HEALTH_QA_QUESTION_FORM_RE`, is `/\?|^(?:does|do|is|are|can|could|should|would|why|how|what|which|when|explain|tell me)\b/i`. Read it plainly: the message needs a question mark in it somewhere, or it has to open with one of those words. There's a broader first check too, `isPureHealthClaimQuestion()`, that also fires on a flat statement if it contains a health-topic word and one of a longer list of "ask" words, true, false, myth, risk, benefit, helps, and a dozen more, so some declarative claims do get caught before the fallback gate even runs. But neither check ever looks at what the coach says back. Both only look at how the user phrased their own side of the conversation. Tell Mr Bite "Sugar spikes your blood glucose, that's why I feel tired after lunch" and the topic is right there, sugar, glucose, but none of the listed ask-words are ("spikes," "feel," and "tired" aren't on either list), there's no question mark, and the sentence opens with "Sugar," not a question word. That turn never reaches the citation system at all, whatever health claim our coach makes back to you.

The fix isn't a new system. We already built the hard part twice over, a vetted source list and a working fallback. It's running the same topic check we already run on the user's words against the coach's own reply too, since `HEALTH_QA_TOPIC_RE.test(assistantText)` is already being computed in the one branch that currently requires it alongside everything else. Washington spent six months failing to define a category and lost an adviser over it. We don't need to define anything. We just need to stop asking only the user's sentence whether it sounds like a question, and start asking the coach's own sentence whether it sounds like a claim.

1. **The CDC is tracking a multistate Salmonella outbreak tied to raw oysters from Virginia's Little Wicomico River harvest area, updated October 19th to 27 cases across 12 states, including 5 hospitalizations.** The agency, working with the FDA and state health departments, says the true case count is almost certainly higher since many people recover without ever being tested. No recall notice has named a specific brand yet; the advice is the standard one for raw shellfish, cook it if you're in a higher-risk group. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/salmonella/outbreaks/oysters-10-26/investigation.html)

2. **Family Farmstead Dairy of Worcester, New York recalled its Raw Mild Cheddar, lot and best-by code 7/22/27, after Maryland state testing turned up two E. coli strains, O26:H11 and O45:H2, in an FDA notice dated October 15th.** The cheese reached retailers in New York, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Connecticut, Maryland, Virginia, and Massachusetts, and the company says it may have traveled further through downstream distributors. No illnesses have been reported; both strains can cause hemolytic uremic syndrome, most dangerous in young children and the elderly. [Fox13 News](https://www.fox13news.com/news/cheddar-cheese-recalled-across-several-states-over-dangerous-e-coli)

No illnesses have been reported; affected pints should be returned for a full refund. ## Tool of the Week: MedlinePlus Connect

![An open reference book of health topics branching into small labeled icons for nutrients and conditions, connecting out to a simple phone app icon, warm cream background with coral and soft teal accents](https://d8j0ntlcm91z4.cloudfront.net/user_3ElLNwQpO0GqKZ9IN6sdAl7YP6i/hf_20261027_082820_163b0099-dda8-4efa-a8d0-62aa14ebf256.png)

Since this issue is about a citation system that leans on MedlinePlus pages as a trusted fallback, the pick this week is the free government web service behind them: MedlinePlus Connect, a documented API from the National Library of Medicine that any app can call to pull back authoritative, plain-language patient information for a given topic or clinical code, no scraping, no guessing which page is current, no risk of linking somewhere that's since gone stale. It won't settle a definitional fight like the one that just cost Kennedy his loudest adviser, nobody's API does that, but for builders who need to cite something real instead of asking a model to remember it, it's exactly the kind of boring, stable infrastructure worth knowing exists. [MedlinePlus Connect](https://medlineplus.gov/connect/overview.html)
