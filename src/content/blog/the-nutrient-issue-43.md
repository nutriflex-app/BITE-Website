On October 5th, Canada's food inspection agency widened a recall that started a month earlier as a single complaint about one bag of snacks. Smarter Snacks' Vegan Protein Puff, Chili & Lime flavor, had been pulled for undeclared milk after a consumer reaction, and the CFIA's first notices named specific lots: best before 11/08/2026, lot SS223; best before 20/04/2027, lot SS110. By October 5th, testing had found the problem wasn't confined to those codes, so the agency rewrote the recall to cover, in its own words, every lot where milk isn't declared on the label. It's rated Class 1, the CFIA's most serious tier, distributed nationally, and the investigation is still open enough that the agency is warning more recalls could follow.

Sit with what that recall actually says for a second. The product's own label was the thing that failed. Not a missing warning sign in a store, not a user mistake, the printed ingredient list itself didn't say milk when the product contained it. For most nutrition information, a wrong label is an inconvenience, your calorie count is off by a bit. For a milk allergy, a wrong label is the only thing standing between a snack and an ER visit, and in this case it already let one reaction through. That's the actual argument this recall makes: if you're building anything that logs what someone eats and claims to care about their allergies, trusting the label text on the package is not a safety feature. It's the exact thing that just failed.

So I went looking for what Mr Bite does when a user has a declared allergy and logs a packaged food. The honest answer surprised me, because it's not "nothing." We have a real allergen safety system, and it's good. `mrBiteExclusions.ts` keeps a hand-built synonym table, `ALLERGEN_SYNONYMS`, that maps each allergen family to the words it actually shows up as: `milk` expands to `dairy`, `cheese`, `butter`, `cream`, `whey`, `casein`, `lactose`, `yogurt`, `ghee`, `curd`, `paneer`, the words a label uses instead of the word "milk." `mrBiteDraftPlan.ts` has `screenRecipeAgainstAllergens`, which checks a recipe's name and its full ingredient list against a user's expanded allergen terms from `profile.allergies`, and `validateSlotRecipe` calls it on every slot in a plan, logging a warning coded `allergen` on a match, or `allergen_unverified` when a recipe has no ingredient list to check at all. This isn't a toy. It's tested, it's wired into `profile.allergies`, and the code comment on it says exactly what it's for: "so a nut recipe whose title never says nut is caught here."

Here's the part that isn't good. That whole system runs in exactly one place: when `mr-bite-chat` drafts a meal plan slot. It is never called from anywhere a user actually brings a packaged product into the app. `openFoodFactsService.ts` is the file that talks to Open Food Facts for both the barcode scanner and the text search screen, and I read it end to end. `lookupOpenFoodFactsByBarcode` fetches a product's full record with no field restriction at all, which means the response that comes back over the wire already contains Open Food Facts' own `allergens_tags` and `traces_tags`, the exact declared-allergen and may-contain fields a recall like this one would show up in. `normalizeOffProduct`, the function that turns that response into what Mr Bite actually stores, reads `product_name`, `brands`, `categories_tags`, every nutriment field down to fiber and sugar, and never once reads `allergens_tags`. It's sitting in the JSON we already paid to fetch, and it's discarded.

The text-search path is worse, because there the data is never even requested. `OFF_SEARCH_V2_FIELDS` and `OFF_SEARCH_CGI_FIELDS` are explicit, hand-picked lists of fields to ask Open Food Facts for, built for speed on a search-results list, and `allergens_tags` isn't in either one. A search hit doesn't just drop the allergen field on the floor, it never asks for it.

Put those two together and the gap is exact: a user with a declared milk allergy who scans or searches for the Smarter Snacks bag mid-recall gets back calories, protein, carbs, and fat, the identical card a user with no allergies at all would see. The one piece of allergy-safety code in this app that could have flagged it, synonym table and all, is never asked the question, because nothing on this path calls it and half of this path doesn't even fetch what it would need to answer.

I don't think the fix here is a new allergen engine. We already built one, and it's more careful than most hand-rolled regexes I've seen, mine included. The fix is wiring: let `screenRecipeAgainstAllergens` (or something that reads the same way) take a scanned or searched product's ingredient text and its `allergens_tags`, and run on the same `profile.allergies` it already reads for meal plans. The hardest part of this problem, writing the synonym table, is done. Right now it only ever gets asked about food Mr Bite itself suggests, never about the food a user found on a shelf.

## Three Quick Bites

![Three vignettes in a row: a pallet of hot dog and deli meat packages beside a small thermometer icon tipped into the red, a wedge of soft cheese beside a glowing bacterium icon, and a carton of ice cream beside a small glinting metal shard icon, warm cream background with coral and soft teal accents](https://d8j0ntlcm91z4.cloudfront.net/user_3ElLNwQpO0GqKZ9IN6sdAl7YP6i/hf_20261025_082008_29b5e014-5cfa-498b-b663-30eff1091137.png)

1. **Kayem Foods recalled about 1,584,361 pounds of ready-to-eat meat and poultry on October 17th after a retail customer's complaint about warm product led the company to a cooling malfunction in its Chelsea, Massachusetts warehouse.** The recall, rated Class 1 by USDA's FSIS, covers hot dogs, bacon, sausage, and deli meats like roast beef and pastrami made between March 31st and October 7th, sold nationwide and in Hong Kong under names including Kayem, Al Fresco, Triple M, and McKenzie. No illnesses have been reported; check your package for establishment numbers "EST. M/P 7839," "EST. P 20935A," or "EST. M 9520." [USDA FSIS](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USFSIS/bulletins/42f8d37)

2. **A multistate E. coli O26:H11 outbreak tied to Graziers raw milk cheese from Sierra Nevada Cheese Company has sickened 13 people across nine states, the CDC says, with 8 hospitalized and 3 who developed hemolytic uremic syndrome.** All nine people interviewed reported eating the cheese, and genetic analysis matched the bacteria across cases; the company recalled every raw milk cheese it makes, medium and sharp cheddar, jalapeño jack, and Monterey jack, after the FDA asked on September 24th. Over half the sick are children five and under. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/ecoli/outbreaks/raw-milk-cheese-09-26/locations.html)

3. **Stewart's Shops recalled two half-gallon ice cream flavors, Heavenly Medley and Vanilla-Chocolate-Strawberry, on October 2nd after a consumer found a piece of metal in a carton.** The New York chain says no injuries have been reported, and shoppers in New York, Vermont, and New Hampshire who bought either flavor between July 17th and October 2nd are asked to return it for a full refund rather than taste-test their way past the problem. [Food Safety News](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2026/10/ice-cream-recalled-because-of-metal-pieces/)

## Tool of the Week: Open Food Facts' allergens taxonomy

![An open reference list of small labeled icons for milk, peanut, egg, and shellfish branching out into smaller tags in different languages, like a family tree of allergen names, warm cream background with coral and soft teal accents](https://d8j0ntlcm91z4.cloudfront.net/user_3ElLNwQpO0GqKZ9IN6sdAl7YP6i/hf_20261025_082009_87524b17-eeff-4519-8ae1-46251113e697.png)

Since this issue is about a hand-built synonym table that's better than most but still ours alone, the pick this week is the free, open, community-maintained list that could extend it: Open Food Facts' own `allergens.txt` taxonomy, the same reference data that powers the `allergens_tags` field our own barcode pipeline already fetches and discards. It maps every EU-regulated allergen (milk, peanuts, tree nuts, sesame, sulfites, and the rest) to its synonyms and translations across dozens of languages, maintained in the open and editable by anyone who finds a gap, which is exactly the kind of coverage a single team's hand-typed list will always lag behind. It won't catch a label that's simply wrong, nothing will, that's what this week's recall is about, but it's a better foundation than reinventing the list from scratch. [allergens.txt on GitHub](https://github.com/openfoodfacts/openfoodfacts-server/blob/main/taxonomies/allergens.txt)
